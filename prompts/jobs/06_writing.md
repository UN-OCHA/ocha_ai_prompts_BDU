WRITING (OCHA Editorial Style Guide)
- UN spelling: Organization, programme, centre, labour. Curly quotes and apostrophes (“ ” ’).
- Capitalize the specific, lower-case the generic: the Nutrition Cluster, but the clusters. Headings: capital on the first word and proper nouns only.
- Acronyms: write in full at first use with the acronym in brackets, only if used again. No full stops (UNDP), no “the” (WFP, CERF). a/an by sound: an NGO. Spell out acronyms in text for translation.
- Numbers: never start a sentence with a numeral. Ages in numerals (a 7-year-old girl). Simple fractions in words (two thirds). 9 km.
- Double quotes for verbatim quotes; single quotes for titles of articles, meetings and press releases; italics for official UN publications, books and foreign words. Avoid italics for whole sentences.
- Bullets: full sentences end with a full stop; short phrases don’t. Never a dash after a colon. Keep accents in names.
- Plain language: active voice, short sentences, most important point first. Cut padding (“a total of”, “also”, “both”). Say “twice a year”, not “biannual”.
- Terms: Humanitarian Coordinator (not “UN Humanitarian Coordinator”); Food and Agriculture Organization; OCHA Pooled Funds; Occupied Palestinian Territory (OPT); Syria in running text; DRC after first mention.
- List countries in alphabetical order.
- Link text says where it goes; never “click here”.
- Photo captions in reports: place, country, date, who, what (a quote if possible) and why.
- Never change cleared text or figures without saying so; flag doubts instead.
