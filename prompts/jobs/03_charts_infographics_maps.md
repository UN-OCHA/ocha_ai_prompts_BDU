CHARTS, INFOGRAPHICS AND MAPS
- Make charts with the Humanitarian DataViz Tool, on brand by default. Web, no install: {{link:dataviz_web}} Illustrator plugin and AI connector: {{link:dataviz_tool_page}}
- Every chart needs a clear message. A single key figure: show it large as text.
- Bars: the default; start at zero; sharp corners; sort largest first; horizontal when labels are long, never rotated labels. Stacked: 3 parts at most. Lines: for trends, 4 lines at most, else small multiples. Pies: up to 5 slices summing to 100%, largest from 12 o’clock. Donuts: two-part data only.
- Label directly instead of legends; no gridlines, or light grey. Natural axis steps (0, 25, 50, 75, 100).
- Colours: UN Blue ramp. HNRP products lead with orange #F58220, Flash Appeals red #ED1847, the GHO yellow #FFC800. Accents only to flag a state.
- Every chart: title with what, where, when and units; source and “as of” date. Footnote any acronym. Rounded parts that don’t add up: “Figures may not add up due to rounding.” More: {{link:numbers}}
- Maps: only if geography adds meaning. Start from OCHA location maps: {{link:location_maps}} . Show neighbouring countries. Circles for counts; blue shading for rates, 5–7 classes, rounded breaks.
- Every standalone map: title, logo, source and date, and the UN disclaimer word for word, at least: “The boundaries and names shown and the designations used on this map do not imply official endorsement or acceptance by the United Nations.” Other cases: {{link:map_disclaimers}}
- Disputed boundaries or territories: never draw them from memory or AI. Follow OCHA’s guidance ({{link:territories}}) or ask ochavisual@un.org.
